Fourier series with phase angles

Sometimes it is desirable to rewrite a general Fourier series as a purely sine or cosine series with phase angles. The new expression can provide a better picture of the amplitude and phase of the motion or signal.

The Fourier series for a periodic function f⁢(t) on the interval [−L,L] is given by

f⁢(t)=a02+∑n=1∞(an⁢cos⁡(n⁢π⁢tL)+bn⁢sin⁡(n⁢π⁢tL)). (1)

We want to re-express the series as

f⁢(t)=a02+∑n=1∞An⁢cos⁡(n⁢π⁢tL+φn). (2)

By the sum formula for cosine,

An⁢cos⁡(n⁢π⁢tL+φn)=An⁢cos⁡n⁢π⁢tL⁢cos⁡φn−An⁢sin⁡n⁢π⁢tL⁢sin⁡φn.

For the RHS of (1) and (2) to be equal, it is necessary that

An⁢cos⁡φn=an,−An⁢sin⁡φn=bn.

Each pair of coefficients (an,bn) gives rise to an amplitude and a phase angle as follows:

An=an2+bn2, (3)
tan⁡φn=−bnan⟹φn=tan−1⁡(−bnan). (4)

For n=1, assume φ1>0.

A1⁢cos⁡(π⁢tL+φ1)=A1⁢cos⁡(πL⁢(t+Lπ⁢φ1)).

Similarly, we can also re-express the Fourier series (1) as a purely sine series,

f⁢(t)=a02+∑n=1∞Bn⁢sin⁡(n⁢π⁢tL+ψn). (5)

By the sum formula for sine,

Bn⁢sin⁡(n⁢π⁢tL+ψn)=Bn⁢sin⁡n⁢π⁢tL⁢cos⁡ψn+Bn⁢cos⁡n⁢π⁢tL⁢sin⁡ψn.

For the RHS of (5) and (1) to be equal, it is necessary that

Bn⁢sin⁡ψn=an,Bn⁢cos⁡ψn=bn.

Therefore, each pair of Fourier coefficients (an,bn) gives rise to an amplitude and a phase angle as follows:

Bn=an2+bn2,
tan⁡ψn=anbn⟹ψn=tan−1⁡(anbn).

Let A0=B0=a0. The sequence {An}n=0∞ or {Bn}n=0∞ is called the frequency spectrum of f⁢(t).

Example 1. Write the Fourier series

f⁢(t)=12+∑n=1∞1n⁢cos⁡(n⁢π⁢tL)−1n⁢sin⁡(n⁢π⁢tL)

in both cosine and sine phase angle form.

For the cosine form,

f⁢(t)=12+∑n=1∞An⁢cos⁡(n⁢π⁢tL+φn),
An=an2+bn2=2n2=2n,
tan⁡φn=−bnan=1,φn=π4⁢ or ⁢5⁢π4.

Note that bn<0, hence φn=π/4. Hence

f⁢(t)=12+2⁢∑n=1∞1n⁢cos⁡(n⁢π⁢tL+π4).

For the sine form,

f⁢(t)=12+∑n=1∞Bn⁢sin⁡(n⁢π⁢tL+ψn),
Bn=2n,tan⁡ψn=anbn=−1,
ψn=3⁢π4⁢ or ⁢7⁢π4.

Note bn<0, hence ψn=3⁢π4:

f⁢(t)=12+∑n=1∞2n⁢sin⁡(n⁢π⁢tL+3⁢π4).

Example 2: Quieting snow tires. Snow tires sometimes produce loud whining noises on dry pavements. Fourier series can be used to analyze the acoustic signals from the tires, and to minimize the noise.

The tire noises result from the interactions between the tread elements and the road surface. As each tread element goes through the contact patch, it generates a pulse of acoustic energy that contributes to the total sound field radiated by the tire. Assume that the tread elements are evenly distributed, and the release of energy is represented by the following figure.

Clearly f⁢(t) is a periodic even function. Its Fourier coefficients can be calculated:

a0=1π⁢[∫−π2−ϵ−π2+ϵ1⁢𝑑t+∫π2−ϵπ2+ϵ1⁢𝑑t]=4⁢ϵπ,
an =1π⁢[∫−π2−ϵ−π2+ϵcos⁡(n⁢t)⁢𝑑t+∫π2−ϵπ2+ϵcos⁡(n⁢t)⁢𝑑t]
=4n⁢π⁢cos⁡(n⁢π2)⁢sin⁡(n⁢ϵ).
f⁢(t)=a02+∑n=1∞an⁢cos⁡(n⁢π⁢tπ)=a02+∑n=1∞an⁢cos⁡(n⁢t). (1)

Set ϵ=π12. The frequency spectrum for the noise of this tire pattern is shown in the following figure.

The frequency spectrum for evenly distributed tread elements has largest values at small n’s (low frequencies). This produces one loud, almost monotonic whine that is undesirable.

To get rid of this monotonic whine, one can change the distribution pattern of the tread elements. We will use just one simple example to demonstrate how the situation can be improved. Of course an optimal solution requires a lot more trials and errors. We consider a periodic but non-even distribution of the tread elements.

a0=1π⁢∫−π2−ϵ−π2+ϵ1⁢𝑑t+∫π4−ϵπ4+ϵ1⁢𝑑t=4⁢ϵπ.
an =1π⁢[∫−π2−ϵ−π2+ϵcos⁡(n⁢t)⁢𝑑t+∫π4−ϵπ4+ϵcos⁡(n⁢t)⁢𝑑t]
=2n⁢π⁢[cos⁡(n⁢π2)+cos⁡(n⁢π4)]⁢sin⁡(n⁢ϵ),
bn =1π⁢[∫−π2−ϵ−π2+ϵsin⁡(n⁢t)⁢𝑑t+∫π4−ϵπ4+ϵsin⁡(n⁢t)⁢𝑑t]
=2n⁢π⁢[sin⁡(n⁢π4)−sin⁡(n⁢π2)]⁢sin⁡(n⁢ϵ).
f⁢(t)=a02+∑n=1∞(an⁢cos⁡n⁢t+bn⁢sin⁡n⁢t). (2)

How to present the frequency spectrum of this case? From previous discussions, we rewrite the Fourier series as a purely sine or cosine series with phase angles,

f⁢(t)=a02+∑n=1∞An⁢cos⁡(n⁢t+φn),An=(an2+bn2)1/2.
