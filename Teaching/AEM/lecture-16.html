The Poisson Equations: the 1D case

We switch the gear in this section, and deal with the Poisson equations, which are basically the Laplace equations but with non-zero right-hand sides. The right-hand side reprsents an external forcing or source, which balances the diffusion process that is represented by the Laplacian term on the left-hand side. To keep things simple at first, we consider the one-dimensional Poisson equation on a line, with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Example. The one-dimensional Poisson equation with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions

Solve the boundary value problem

{u′′⁢(x)=f⁢(x),0<x<L,u⁢(0)=u⁢(L)=0.
Solution.

The basic idea is to find the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions for the associated Sturm-Liouville problem,

{u′′+λ⁢u=0,0<x<L,u⁢(0)=u⁢(L)=0,

and then use these eigenfunctions as building blocks to construct the solution to the original problem (1). Thus the this boundary value problem (1) for Poisson equation can be solved in just two steps.

Step 1. Solve the Sturm–Liouville problem for u.

Using the methods we learned in the last chapter, we find the eigenvalues and corresponding eigenfunctions of the S-L problem (2) to be

λm=(m⁢πL)2,um⁢(x)=sin⁡(m⁢π⁢xL),m≥1.

Step 2. Assemble the solution to the Poisson equation (1).

We write the solution as a series,

u⁢(x)=∑m=1∞Am⁢um⁢(x)=∑m=1∞Am⁢sin⁡(m⁢π⁢xL).

Every term satisfies the homogeneous boundary conditions. Substituting this series into (1), and using the eigenvalue equation, we obtain

−∑m=1∞λm⁢Am⁢sin⁡(m⁢π⁢xL)=f⁢(x).

To determine the coefficients Am, we first set

fm=−λm⁢Am,m≥1.

Then

∑m=1∞fm⁢sin⁡(m⁢π⁢xL)=f⁢(x).

This is just a Fourier sine series expansion for the source term f⁢(x). Its coefficients are

fm=2L⁢∫0Lf⁢(x)⁢sin⁡(m⁢π⁢xL)⁢𝑑x,m≥1.

Consequently,

Am=−fmλm=−2⁢Lm2⁢π2∫0Lf(x)sin(m⁢π⁢xL)dx,m≥1.

The solution is therefore given by (3), with Am given by (4). Equivalently,
