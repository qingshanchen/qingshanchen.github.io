Exercises on elliptic boundary value problems

Exercise 1. One-dimensional Poisson equation

Solve the 1D Poisson equation with nonhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and the corrected right-hand side:

{u′′⁢(x)=sin⁡(2⁢x),0<x<π,u⁢(0)=1,u⁢(π)=2.

Solution: splitting and superposition

By linearity, split the forcing from the nonhomogeneous boundary data into two subproblems and write

u=u1+u2.

The superscripts label sub-solutions; they are not powers.

Subproblem u1: the forcing

{(u1)′′⁢(x)=sin⁡(2⁢x),0<x<π,u1⁢(0)=0,u1⁢(π)=0.

The Dirichlet eigenfunctions of −d2/d⁢x2 are sin⁡(n⁢x), with eigenvalues n2. Expanding u1=∑n≥1an⁢sin⁡(n⁢x) and using orthogonality gives

−n2⁢an=2π⁢∫0πsin⁡(2⁢x)⁢sin⁡(n⁢x)⁢𝑑x={1,n=2,0,n≠2.

Thus a2=−1/4 and all other coefficients vanish:

u1(x)=−14sin(2x).

Subproblem u2: the boundary values

{(u2)′′⁢(x)=0,0<x<π,u2⁢(0)=1,u2⁢(π)=2.

The equation gives u2⁢(x)=A+B⁢x. Imposing the endpoint values yields A=1 and B=1/π, so

u2(x)=1+xπ.

Adding the two sub-solutions gives

u(x)=u1(x)+u2(x)=1+xπ−14sin(2x).

Exercise 2. Two-dimensional Poisson equation

Solve the 2D Poisson equation with nonhomogeneous boundary conditions:

{ux⁢x+uy⁢y=cos⁡x⁢sin⁡(2⁢y),0<x<π,0<y<π,ux⁢(0,y)=1,ux⁢(π,y)=0,0<y<π,u⁢(x,0)=0,u⁢(x,π)=x,0<x<π.

Here ux denotes the partial derivative in the coordinate variable x.

Solution: splitting and superposition

Let Ω=(0,π)×(0,π). Split the source, the left boundary derivative, and the top boundary value into three subproblems:

u=u1+u2+u3.

In each subproblem, retain one nonzero datum and set all the other data to zero. Boundary conditions below are imposed on the open sides.

Subproblem u1: the forcing

{Δ⁢u1=cos⁡x⁢sin⁡(2⁢y),(x,y)∈Ω,(u1)x⁢(0,y)=0,(u1)x⁢(π,y)=0,0<y<π,u1⁢(x,0)=0,u1⁢(x,π)=0,0<x<π.

The corresponding eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of −Δ are

ϕm⁢n⁢(x,y)=cos⁡(m⁢x)⁢sin⁡(n⁢y),λm⁢n=m2+n2,m≥0,n≥1.

The corrected forcing is exactly ϕ12=cos⁡x⁢sin⁡(2⁢y), with eigenvalue 12+22=5. In the expansion

u1⁢(x,y)=∑m=0∞∑n=1∞Am⁢n⁢cos⁡(m⁢x)⁢sin⁡(n⁢y),

coefficient matching gives

−(m2+n2)⁢Am⁢n={1,(m,n)=(1,2),0,otherwise.

Thus the expansion reduces to one term:

u1(x,y)=−15cosxsin(2y).

Indeed, Δ⁢u1=cos⁡x⁢sin⁡(2⁢y) and (u1)x=15⁢sin⁡x⁢sin⁡(2⁢y) vanishes on both vertical sides; u1 vanishes on both horizontal sides. No boundary correction to u1 is needed.

Subproblem u2: the left boundary derivative

{Δ⁢u2=0,(x,y)∈Ω,(u2)x⁢(0,y)=1,(u2)x⁢(π,y)=0,0<y<π,u2⁢(x,0)=0,u2⁢(x,π)=0,0<x<π.

Separation of variables. Seek a nonzero product X⁢(x)⁢Y⁢(y). Substitution into Δ⁢u2=0 gives

X′′⁢Y+X⁢Y′′=0,X′′X=−Y′′Y=λ.

The homogeneous conditions on the horizontal sides lead to the eigenvalue problem

Y′′+λ⁢Y=0,Y⁢(0)=Y⁢(π)=0,

while X′′−λ⁢X=0. If λ=0, then Y=A+B⁢y, and both endpoint conditions force Y=0. If λ=−μ2<0, then Y=A⁢cosh⁡(μ⁢y)+B⁢sinh⁡(μ⁢y), and again Y=0. For λ=k2>0,

Y⁢(y)=A⁢cos⁡(k⁢y)+B⁢sin⁡(k⁢y),A=0,B⁢sin⁡(k⁢π)=0.

Nonzero solutions require k=n, n=1,2,…. Thus

λn=n2,Yn(y)=sin(ny),n≥1.

Expansion in eigenfunctions and fundamental solutions. For each eigenvalue, the equation Xn′′−n2⁢Xn=0 has the fundamental solutions cosh⁡(n⁢x) and sinh⁡(n⁢x). Superposition therefore gives

u2⁢(x,y)=∑n=1∞[An⁢cosh⁡(n⁢x)+Bn⁢sinh⁡(n⁢x)]⁢sin⁡(n⁢y).

Determine An and Bn directly from the boundary conditions. Differentiate the expansion with respect to x:

(u2)x⁢(x,y)=∑n=1∞n⁢[An⁢sinh⁡(n⁢x)+Bn⁢cosh⁡(n⁢x)]⁢sin⁡(n⁢y).

At x=0, the condition (u2)x⁢(0,y)=1 becomes

∑n=1∞n⁢Bn⁢sin⁡(n⁢y)=1,0<y<π.

Multiply by sin⁡(k⁢y) and integrate over (0,π). Orthogonality gives

k⁢Bk⁢π2=∫0πsin⁡(k⁢y)⁢𝑑y=1−(−1)kk.

Relabeling k as n yields

Bn=2⁢(1−(−1)n)π⁢n2={4π⁢n2,n⁢ odd,0,n⁢ even.

At x=π, impose (u2)x⁢(π,y)=0 directly:

∑n=1∞n⁢[An⁢sinh⁡(n⁢π)+Bn⁢cosh⁡(n⁢π)]⁢sin⁡(n⁢y)=0.

Orthogonality forces every coefficient to vanish. Since n≥1,

An⁢sinh⁡(n⁢π)+Bn⁢cosh⁡(n⁢π)=0,

so

An=−Bn⁢coth⁡(n⁢π)={−4⁢coth⁡(n⁢π)π⁢n2,n⁢ odd,0,n⁢ even.

Substituting An and Bn directly into the expansion gives

u2(x,y)=∑n≥1n⁢ odd[−4⁢coth⁡(n⁢π)π⁢n2cosh(nx)+4π⁢n2sinh(nx)]sin(ny).

Subproblem u3: the top boundary value

{Δ⁢u3=0,(x,y)∈Ω,(u3)x⁢(0,y)=0,(u3)x⁢(π,y)=0,0<y<π,u3⁢(x,0)=0,u3⁢(x,π)=x,0<x<π.

This subproblem can be solved similarly by separation of variables, now using the Neumann eigenfunctions 1,cos⁡(m⁢x) in x. The corresponding fundamental solutions in y are 1,y for the zero mode and cosh⁡(m⁢y),sinh⁡(m⁢y) for m≥1. Expanding the top datum x and matching the boundary conditions gives

u3(x,y)=y2−4π∑n≥1n⁢ oddsinh⁡(n⁢y)n2⁢sinh⁡(n⁢π)cos(nx).

Combined solution

By superposition, u=u1+u2+u3, so
