The Secant Method

The Newton method locate the next approximation to the root by drawing a tangent line through a known point on the function curve; see below.

f⁢(x)=0
xk+1=xk−f⁢(x)f′⁢(xk),k≥0.

The slope of the function curve, or derivative of the function, is required for this purpose, which is a main drawback of the Newton method. What if the derivative is not available? This can indeed happen in reality when the function is either not available analytically, or is just too complex to differentiate. In the absence of the exact slope, one may draw a secant line through two adjacent points on the function curve. provided that the two points used for the drawing are not too far apar, the secant line provides a decent approximation of the function curve in the nearby region, and thus can be used to locate the next approximation of the root of the function (see below).

The resulting method, as a modification to the Newton’s Method, is called the Secant Method. It bypasses the requirement of the slope or the function derivative.

Let’s now work out the mechanics of the Secant Method. Assume xk−1 & xk are available, but the derivative f′ is NOT available. We can approximate derivative at xk by the slope of the secant line passing through (xk−1,f⁢(xk−1)) and (xk,f⁢(xk)),

f′⁢(xk)≈f⁢(xk−1)−f⁢(xk)xk−1−xk

Now we replace f′⁢(xk) by the slope of the secant line in Newton’s formula,

xk+1 =xk−f⁢(xk)f⁢(xk−1)−f⁢(xk)xk−1−xk
=xk−(xk−1−xk)⁢f⁢(xk)f⁢(xk−1)−f⁢(xk)
=xk⁢f⁢(xk−1)−xk⁢f⁢(xk)−xk−1⁢f⁢(xk)+xk⁢f⁢(xk)f⁢(xk−1)−f⁢(xk)
=xk⁢f⁢(xk−1)−xk−1⁢f⁢(xk)f⁢(xk−1)−f⁢(xk).

This iterative formula embodies the Secant Method. It yieds the next approximation xk+1 to the true root x∗, from two previous approximations xk−1 and xk.

Algorithm (The secant method)

Given f, tol, x0, x1

while ⁢(|xk−xk−1|>tol):
xk+1=xk−1⁢f⁢(xk)−xk⁢f⁢(xk−1)f⁢(xk)−f⁢(xk−1)

Comparison of bisection, Newton, and Secant methods

Definition 10.1.

Let en be the error in step n of an algorithm. We say an iteration converges with order k if

limk→∞|en+1||en|k=C<∞,

where C is a constant that does not depend on n. If k=1 we call the convergence “linear”, if k=2 “quadratic”.

Applying this definition to the few methods we have learned so far, we find that

Bisection: |en+1|≈12⁢|en|,k=1,1st-order (linear) convergence
Newton: |en+1|≈C⋅|en|2,k=2,2nd-order (quadratic) convergence
Secant: |en+1|≈C⁢|en|k,k=1+52≈1.6,superlinear convergence.

As a second order accurate iterative algorithm, the Newton’s method pulls far ahead in terms of accuracy, followed by the Secant Method. However, the Newton’s method is not an option when the functions are not available in differentiable analytic forms. In the real world, people use a combination of bisection (for reliability) and secant (for performance) methods.
