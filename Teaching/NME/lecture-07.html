At the checking stage in the above, if a singular (small) diagonal entry is encountered, there are two strategies for the pivoting:

  1. 1.

    Go down the list, and do a swapping with the first row whose leading entry is above the threshold.

  2. 2.

    Find the row whose leading entry has the largest abs. value. Do the swapping.

The second strategy is generally perferred, because it brings the largest possible values to the diagonal, leading to smaller multiplication factors, and an overall more stable algorithm. Below is a pseudocode for the second strategy.

Some tips on Matlab coding

  1. 1.

    How to find the largest value in a vector, together with its index? Suppose a is a vector.

    ⬇
    max(a)
    [m,i] = max(a)

    m stores the max value, and i the index of the entry of max val.

  2. 2.

    Once a candidate row has been found, how to do a swapping?

    Suppose a,b are two variables (scalar or vectorial). Bring in another temporary variable c,

    ⬇
    c=a;
    a=b;
    b=c;

    If the two vectors to be swapped are part of a matrix A, with row indices i and j, to swap these two rows, Matlab allows the following coding style,

    ⬇
    A([i,j],:) = A([j,i],:);
